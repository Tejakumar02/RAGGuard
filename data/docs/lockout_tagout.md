# Lockout/Tagout (LOTO) Procedure

This document is part of the fictional Kestrel Motors Plant 4 operations handbook, written as sample data for RAGGuard.

## When LOTO is required

Lockout/tagout is required before any maintenance, cleaning or jam clearing that puts a body part inside a machine guard. It is also required when a conveyor, press or robot cell must be stopped for longer than 10 minutes for repair. Quick adjustments made from outside the guard with the machine in safe-stop mode do not require LOTO.

## Lockout steps

First, notify every operator in the affected cell that the equipment is being shut down. Second, stop the machine with its normal stop control. Third, open the main disconnect and isolate all energy sources, including electrical, pneumatic and hydraulic supplies. Fourth, attach a personal red padlock and a signed danger tag to each isolation point. Finally, press the start button to verify zero energy before starting work.

## Locks and keys

Each authorized employee receives one red padlock with a single key. The key must stay with the employee for the whole job and may never be handed to someone else. When several people work on the same machine, each person applies their own lock to a group lockout hasp.

## Removing locks

Only the person who applied a lock may remove it. If that person has left the site, the shift supervisor may remove the lock only after phoning the employee and completing form SF-12 with a second witness.

## Training

LOTO training is mandatory for all maintenance staff and must be refreshed every 12 months. New hires may not perform lockout until they pass the practical assessment.
